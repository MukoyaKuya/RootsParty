Executive Summary

"Hemp is Economy." This policy brief outlines the Roots Party's strategic vision for a regulated, profitable, and responsible cannabis industry in Kenya.

Kenya stands at a critical economic junction. We possess the ideal equatorial climate, rich volcanic soil, and a skilled agricultural workforce necessary to dominate the global legal cannabis market. Yet, we remain on the sidelines while the global industry surges past $50 Billion.

Our proposal goes beyond mere legalization; it is a framework for National Economic Sovereignty. By commercializing industrial hemp and medicinal marijuana, we aim to:

  • Eradicate National Debt: Direct taxation and export duties will generate the projected Ksh 300 Billion annually needed to service our sovereign debt.
  • Revitalize Manufacturing: From hemp textiles to biodegradable plastics and low-cost construction materials (Hempcrete), we will spark an industrial revolution.
  • Advance Public Health: We will provide affordable, locally-produced medicinal cannabis for patients suffering from cancer, epilepsy, and chronic pain.

This framework ensures strict adherence to global best practices, prioritizing public safety, child protection, and rigorous quality control. We invite all Kenyans to envision a future where our natural resources fuel our prosperity.

Prof. George Wajackoyah, Party Leader

Understanding the Difference

Hemp vs. Marijuana: Two Different Plants

It is critical to understand that industrial hemp and recreational marijuana are fundamentally different, despite coming from the same plant species (Cannabis sativa). The key difference is THC content.

🌿

HEMP

Industrial Use

✓ THC Content: <0.3%
✓ Non-psychoactive (cannot get you "high")
✓ 50,000+ industrial applications
✓ Legal in 50+ countries worldwide

Uses: Textiles, paper, bioplastics, construction (Hempcrete), biofuel, food (hemp seeds, oil), animal feed, cosmetics.

💊

MARIJUANA

Medical/Recreational

✓ THC Content: 5-30%
✓ Psychoactive (produces "high")
✓ Requires strict regulation
✓ Age-restricted (21+ under our proposal)

Uses: Medical treatment (epilepsy, chronic pain, cancer symptoms), controlled recreational use with licensing.

Key Point: Our policy proposes immediate industrialization of hemp (0.3% THC limit aligned with international standards) while implementing carefully regulated medicinal/recreational marijuana through strict licensing.

Implementation Challenges & Solutions

Addressing Realistic Obstacles

⚖️ Legal Hurdles

Challenge: Kenya's Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances Act (1994) criminalizes cannabis.

Challenge: UN Single Convention on Narcotic Drugs (1961) obligations.

Solution: Propose Cannabis (Regulation and Control) Bill with hemp carve-out. Many UN signatories (Uruguay, Canada, Germany) have successfully legislated within treaty flexibility.

🙏 Cultural/Religious Opposition

Challenge: Strong opposition from religious communities and conservative voters.

Solution: Lead with industrial hemp (non-psychoactive), emphasize medical benefits, engage religious leaders with economic data, and respect community opt-out provisions for counties.

🌍 International Trade Barriers

Challenge: Export restrictions to countries where cannabis remains illegal.

Solution: Focus on legal markets (EU, Canada, Australia), hemp products have fewer restrictions, build bilateral agreements like Lesotho-Canada model.

🔒 Enforcement & Diversion

Challenge: Risk of legal cannabis diverting to illegal markets or minors.

Solution: Seed-to-sale tracking system, mandatory licensing, strict penalties, partnership with NPS and county enforcement units.

Public Health & Safety Framework

Protecting Kenyans: Health First Approach

Responsible cannabis policy requires robust public health protections. We commit to evidence-based approaches aligned with WHO guidelines.

🛡️ Youth Protection

  • • 21+ age restriction (higher than alcohol at 18)
  • • School education programs on risks
  • • Heavy penalties for selling to minors (license revocation + prosecution)
  • • No advertising near schools, playgrounds, or youth facilities
  • • ID verification at all points of sale

🧠 Addiction Prevention

  • • Mandatory health warnings on all products
  • • Free addiction helpline (24/7)
  • • Funded treatment centers in every county
  • • Regular public awareness campaigns
  • • Monitoring and research on usage patterns

✅ Quality & Safety

  • • KEBS-certified testing for all products
  • • Lab testing for pesticides, mold, THC content
  • • Childproof packaging requirements
  • • No driving under influence laws + testing
  • • Medical professional training programs

Evidence-Based: Our health framework is modeled on WHO recommendations, Colorado's successful approach (youth use rates remained stable post-legalization), and Portugal's public health-centered drug policy.

Social Equity & Inclusion

Building an Inclusive Industry

Cannabis legalization must not repeat the mistakes of other industries where large corporations dominate while small farmers and marginalized communities are left behind. Our policy prioritizes equity.

🌾 Small Farmer Support Program

  • 1 Subsidized Licensing: 50% reduced fees for small-scale farmers (<5 acres)
  • 2 Free Training Programs: County-level agricultural extension for hemp cultivation
  • 3 Cooperative Formation: Support for farmer cooperatives to access markets
  • 4 Access to Seeds: Government seed bank for certified hemp varieties

👩‍🌾 Youth & Women Inclusion

  • 1 30% Youth Quota: Reserved processing and retail licenses for under-35s
  • 2 Women's Hemp Fund: Low-interest loans for women-led cannabis businesses
  • 3 TVET Integration: Cannabis agriculture curriculum in polytechnics
  • 4 County Equity Boards: Local oversight to ensure diverse participation

⚖️ Criminal Justice Reform

Record Expungement

Automatic expungement of convictions for simple possession (non-trafficking). Free legal clinics to assist with applications.

Priority Licensing

Those previously convicted for cannabis will receive priority consideration for cultivation licenses—turning former "criminals" into licensed entrepreneurs.

Regulatory Framework

Cannabis Control Authority of Kenya (CCAK)

The Cannabis Control Authority of Kenya (CCAK) will be established as an independent regulatory body under the Ministry of Agriculture. CCAK will oversee all aspects of the cannabis industry—from seed to sale—ensuring that Kenya develops a safe, transparent, and economically beneficial cannabis sector while preventing abuse and protecting public health.

Core Functions

✓ Issue and monitor all licenses
✓ Enforce quality and safety standards
✓ Prevent diversion to illegal markets
✓ Ensure fair taxation and revenue collection
✓ Protect youth through strict age restrictions (21+)
✓ Regulate advertising and marketing
